## New tests

File: [companion-outfits-rules.test.ts](../../../apps/web/lib/companion-outfits-rules.test.ts). All seven pass (TAP `ok 92` to `ok 98`).

1. Each outfit is one layer with its own drawing. The seven files have distinct SHA-256 digests, and the outfits folder holds exactly those seven SVGs.
2. SVG hygiene beyond [companion-outfits.test.ts](../../../apps/web/lib/companion-outfits.test.ts): each file is under 20 KB, every `href` points at an id inside the same file and that id exists, and there are no `on*` event handler attributes.
3. Cosmetic only: `outfits.ts` and `rig-renderer.ts`, with comments removed, have no identifier containing mastery, grade, progress, star or stars, xp, coin or coins, supabase or fetch as a word.
4. Control for test 3: `masteryLevel`, `xp_total` and `starCount` are caught; `gradient`, `restart` and `startAt` are not.
5. Picker: the 15 lines that mention an outfit (11 in `engine.ts`, 4 in `companion.tsx`) pass test 3. The picker's copy line, "Cosmetic only. It never changes his progress.", is set aside. The select and its change handler write only the outfit preference.
6. Random frames: 2,000 frames from a real director with a seeded generator, driven by 173 scripted calls to `onEvent`, `lookAt`, `setHeld` and `setThinking`. For every outfit, each pose is finite, x and y are inside -200 to 600 rig px, `|scaleX|` and `|scaleY|` are at most 2, and `|rotate|` is at most 1 rad.
7. Prefs: unknown outfit ids in saved JSON read back as `null`, and every real id round-trips through `parsePrefs`. This overlaps the malformed-outfit test in `companion-logic.test.ts`; it sits here so the outfit rules are in one file.

## Per-outfit CPU frame-time delta (Node, CPU only)

Each round uses one director and one mesh with the same seed and script. Pass A times `director.advance` plus `poseMesh` alone (the baseline). Pass B runs the same frames in the renderer's order: after `poseMesh`, it times `outfitTransform` for each outfit, 20 calls per span. 5 rounds of 20,000 timed frames follow 3,000 warm-up frames, giving 100,000 samples per column. A frame with an outfit is baseline plus outfit for the same frame index. Delta p95 is p95 of the frame with the outfit minus p95 of the baseline. Times are in microseconds.

| Configuration | Frame mean us | Frame p95 us | Delta mean us | Delta p95 us | Round means us (min to max) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| baseline (advance + poseMesh) | 86.00 | 175.00 | +0.00 | +0.00 | frame 55.82 to 117.04 |
| goggles | 86.56 | 175.49 | +0.56 | +0.49 | outfit call 0.38 to 0.76 |
| explorer | 86.36 | 175.33 | +0.36 | +0.33 | outfit call 0.25 to 0.47 |
| visor | 86.55 | 175.53 | +0.55 | +0.53 | outfit call 0.39 to 0.71 |
| reader | 86.55 | 175.50 | +0.55 | +0.50 | outfit call 0.38 to 0.75 |
| beret | 86.36 | 175.34 | +0.36 | +0.33 | outfit call 0.25 to 0.47 |
| headphones | 86.37 | 175.34 | +0.38 | +0.33 | outfit call 0.25 to 0.50 |
| sash | 86.17 | 175.19 | +0.17 | +0.19 | outfit call 0.12 to 0.23 |

Reading the table: each outfit adds 0.17 to 0.56 us mean per frame in Node, about 0.003% of a 16,667 us frame at 60 Hz. Eye-slot outfits cost the most because they take two head-point poses, and the neck slot (sash) costs the least because it takes one chest pose, which matches [outfits.ts](../../../apps/web/lib/companion-motion/outfits.ts). The round-to-round spread of the baseline (56 to 117 us) is laptop noise. It does not enter the deltas, which come from the outfit spans in the same frames, but per-round outfit means still move by up to about 0.4 us, so treat the ranking between outfits as rough.

Not measured: GPU and compositor cost of the overlay `<img>`, style recalculation, browser main-thread cost, and iPhone or Android frame time. The device rows of [LC-1.md](LC-1.md) stay OPEN (human).

Method notes: an earlier attempt timed the outfit calls inside the baseline loop, and the baseline rose to 277 us mean, so the two passes are kept apart. Another attempt ran each configuration as its own pass, and identical baseline rows differed by 27 us, so it was dropped. Neither is in the table.
