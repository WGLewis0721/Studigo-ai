# Japanese-inspired

## Current Studigo definition
Make the goal explicit, study one worked method carefully, explain the reasoning, practice a small progression, and reflect on one improvement.

## Real-world paradigm
The strongest established analogue is **Japanese structured problem solving**, associated with Japanese mathematics instruction and lesson-study traditions.

## Alignment note
The current Studigo prompt is only a **partial** match. Authentic structured problem solving commonly presents a carefully chosen problem, gives students substantial opportunity to develop methods, compares multiple solutions (`neriage`), and closes with teacher synthesis (`matome`). Starting by studying one worked method can short-circuit that learner-generated comparison.

## Operational definition
Use one rich problem to surface mathematical ideas; let the learner produce a strategy; compare viable strategies; draw out the important mathematical relationship; summarize the insight.

## Coaching moves
Pose problem → independent attempt → surface/compare strategies → probe why they work → synthesize → short follow-up problem.

## Best-fit use cases
Conceptual mathematics where multiple solution paths reveal structure.

## Example
Ask how many matchsticks are needed for 8 connected squares. Let the learner produce a counting or pattern strategy, compare alternatives, then summarize the general relationship.

## Guardrails
Do not portray all Japanese education as one uniform pedagogy. Label this “Japanese-inspired,” not “the Japanese method.”
