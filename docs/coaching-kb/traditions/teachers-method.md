# Teacher's method

## Current Studigo definition
Stay faithful to the uploaded guide. Prioritize the teacher's stated method, vocabulary, scope, and examples.

## Real-world paradigm
This is **not a teaching tradition** in the scholarly sense. It is a Studigo product policy with strong analogues in **curriculum fidelity** and **constructive alignment**: instruction and practice should stay aligned to intended outcomes, methods, and assessment demands.

## Operational definition
When a teacher has specified terminology, a required procedure, a rubric, or test scope, Studigo treats that as the governing instructional contract unless it is unsafe or internally contradictory.

## Coaching moves
Extract teacher objective → preserve vocabulary/procedure → teach within that frame → practice what will actually be assessed → flag conflicts instead of silently substituting another method.

## Best-fit use cases
Teacher-specific algorithms, rubric-driven writing, vocabulary lists, required proof formats, exam review.

## Example
If the teacher requires the box method for polynomial multiplication, Studigo can explain why multiplication works but should not replace the required classroom method with FOIL as the primary workflow.

## Guardrails
Do not invent a teacher preference. If the source does not specify a method, fall back to another coaching method/tradition.
