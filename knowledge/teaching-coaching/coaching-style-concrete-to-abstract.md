# Concrete to Abstract → CPA / CRA

## Current Studigo definition

**Description:** Models, diagrams, then symbols.

**Instruction:** "Start with a concrete or visual model, connect it to the idea, and only then move to symbolic or abstract practice. Keep the teacher's terminology."

## Real-world alignment

This closely matches the **Concrete–Pictorial–Abstract (CPA)** sequence widely associated with Singapore mathematics and the closely related **Concrete–Representational–Abstract (CRA)** terminology used in other mathematics education literature.

## Definition

The learner encounters a mathematical relationship through tangible objects or actions, then through visual/diagrammatic representation, and finally through abstract notation. The important mechanism is the explicit connection among representations.

## Core sequence

1. Identify the mathematical relationship.
2. Concrete: manipulate or describe tangible quantities.
3. Pictorial/representational: show the same relation with bars, arrays, number lines, drawings, or diagrams.
4. Explicitly connect each visual element to the quantity or action.
5. Abstract: express the same relationship with symbols.
6. Move backward if symbols lose meaning.
7. Test with a new example.

## Best-fit use cases

- Place value and regrouping.
- Fractions.
- Ratios and proportions.
- Multiplication/division arrays.
- Word problems and bar models.
- Early algebraic relationships.

## Cautions

- The strongest fit is mathematics; do not force CPA into subjects where "concrete" has no coherent meaning.
- Decorative visuals are not a representational bridge.
- Do not remain in the concrete phase once the learner can reason symbolically.
- The phases need not be a rigid one-way staircase.

## Example

For `43 - 18`, Studigo represents 43 as tens and ones, exchanges one ten for ten ones, draws the exchange, then connects it to the written regrouping notation before asking the learner to solve a new subtraction problem.

## Recommended coaching rules

- Label what each object or diagram represents.
- Ask the learner to explain the connection between representations.
- Preserve the teacher's vocabulary and notation.
- Use representation to repair meaning, not as decoration.
- Move to abstract work when the learner can explain the mapping.
