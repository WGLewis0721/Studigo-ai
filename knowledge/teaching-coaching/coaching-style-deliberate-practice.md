# Deliberate Practice

## Current Studigo definition

**Description:** Targeted reps that build automaticity.

**Instruction:** "Use deliberate practice when it fits: isolate one skill, give a carefully targeted sequence of up to 50 problems one at a time, vary difficulty gradually, give immediate specific feedback, and revisit errors. Do not assign busywork."

## Real-world alignment

This is a strong match to the deliberate-practice framework: practice activities are chosen specifically to improve a defined component of performance, require effortful attention, and are paired with informative feedback.

The current "up to 50 problems" is a product limit, not a research-derived dose. Quantity alone does not make practice deliberate.

## Definition

Deliberate Practice is structured practice designed specifically to improve current performance on a well-defined skill. It targets weaknesses, keeps difficulty productive, provides feedback, and changes the next attempt based on what happened.

## Core sequence

1. Define a measurable target.
2. Diagnose the weakest component.
3. Select a task just beyond reliable performance.
4. Require an attempt.
5. Give specific feedback.
6. Retry or vary the task.
7. Increase challenge after demonstrated control.
8. Revisit prior errors later.

## Best-fit use cases

- Arithmetic procedures.
- Algebraic transformations.
- Grammar rules.
- Pronunciation.
- Technical procedures.
- Any skill with observable performance and reliable feedback.

## Cautions

- Repetition without diagnosis and feedback is ordinary drill, not deliberate practice.
- If the learner does not understand the underlying concept, route to explicit instruction or scaffolding.
- Expertise is not explained by practice alone; effects vary by domain.
- Do not infer that longer sessions are better.

## Example

A learner repeatedly mishandles subtraction with regrouping. Studigo isolates that decision, presents a short sequence requiring regrouping, corrects the exact place-value error, then mixes in items where regrouping is unnecessary so the learner must discriminate when to apply it.

## Recommended coaching rules

- Target one observable weakness at a time.
- Keep feedback specific enough to change the next attempt.
- Stop and reteach if the same conceptual error repeats.
- Vary surface details after initial stability.
- Require a transfer check before increasing difficulty.
