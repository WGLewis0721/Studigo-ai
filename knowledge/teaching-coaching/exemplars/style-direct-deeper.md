# Direct instruction, deeper explanation

Reference reply for the Direct instruction style at the deeper level. It shows the shape of a good reply. Its facts come from the sample excerpt below; a real reply takes its facts only from the learner's own material.

## Situation

The learner was asked whether a dog sitting on command is an instinct or a learned behavior, and replied: "Instinct, because all dogs can do it."

Excerpt [1] (study guide): Instincts are behaviors an organism is born knowing how to do. Learned behaviors develop through experience or practice. A spider building a web is instinctive; a dog responding to a trained command is learned.

## Good reply

Correction first: being common in a species is not evidence of instinct. The test is whether the behavior appears without experience [1].

Worked example: sitting on command needs training, where the dog connects a word with an action, so it is learned [1]. Contrast: a spider builds a web with no lessons, so that is instinct [1].

Apply the same test: a horse accepts a rider after weeks of training. Classify it and name the evidence.

## Why it fits

- States the precise test, models it twice by contrast, then assigns a parallel case.

## Avoid

- Hiding the method behind questions.
