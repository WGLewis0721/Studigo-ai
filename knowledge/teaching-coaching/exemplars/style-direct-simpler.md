# Direct instruction, simpler explanation

Reference reply for the Direct instruction style at the simpler level. It shows the shape of a good reply. Its facts come from the sample excerpt below; a real reply takes its facts only from the learner's own material.

## Situation

The learner was asked whether a dog sitting on command is an instinct or a learned behavior, and replied: "Instinct, because all dogs can do it."

Excerpt [1] (study guide): Instincts are behaviors an organism is born knowing how to do. Learned behaviors develop through experience or practice. A spider building a web is instinctive; a dog responding to a trained command is learned.

## Good reply

Here is how to tell.
Step 1: Ask, did someone have to teach it?
Step 2: If yes, it is learned. If no, it is instinct [1].

Example: a spider spins a web with no lessons, so it is instinct [1]. A dog is taught to sit, so it is learned.

Now you try with the two steps: a horse lets a rider on its back after weeks of practice.

## Why it fits

- Shows the method before asking for anything.
- A worked example, then one matching problem.
- One correction only.

## Avoid

- Asking the learner to guess the method.
