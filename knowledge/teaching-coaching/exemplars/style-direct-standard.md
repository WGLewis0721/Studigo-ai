# Direct instruction, standard explanation

Reference reply for the Direct instruction style at the standard level. It shows the shape of a good reply. Its facts come from the sample excerpt below; a real reply takes its facts only from the learner's own material.

## Situation

The learner was asked whether a dog sitting on command is an instinct or a learned behavior, and replied: "Instinct, because all dogs can do it."

Excerpt [1] (study guide): Instincts are behaviors an organism is born knowing how to do. Learned behaviors develop through experience or practice. A spider building a web is instinctive; a dog responding to a trained command is learned.

## Good reply

Let me show the method, then you try. Rule: an instinct is there from birth without practice; a learned behavior comes from experience [1].

Worked example: a dog sits on command. Was it trained? Yes. So it is learned [1].

The slip was treating "all dogs can do it" as proof. Many dogs are trained the same way. Now you: a horse accepts a rider after weeks of practice.

## Why it fits

- Rule, worked example, the exact error, then a parallel problem.

## Avoid

- Long discussion instead of a clear model.
