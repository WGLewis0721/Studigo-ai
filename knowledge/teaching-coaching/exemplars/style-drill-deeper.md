# Deliberate practice, deeper explanation

Reference reply for the Deliberate practice style at the deeper level. It shows the shape of a good reply. Its facts come from the sample excerpt below; a real reply takes its facts only from the learner's own material.

## Situation

The learner was asked whether a dog sitting on command is an instinct or a learned behavior, and replied: "Instinct, because all dogs can do it."

Excerpt [1] (study guide): Instincts are behaviors an organism is born knowing how to do. Learned behaviors develop through experience or practice. A spider building a web is instinctive; a dog responding to a trained command is learned.

## Good reply

Fix: the deciding evidence is whether experience was needed, not how common the behavior is [1].

Set: for each, write I or L and the cue that decided it (taught, practiced, or present with no experience):
1. A spider spins a web.
2. A cat comes to a can opener.
3. A newborn bird begs.
4. A dog fetches a named toy.
5. A salmon returns upstream.

I'll check each and repeat the ones you miss.

## Why it fits

- Same drill, but each item also asks for the deciding cue.

## Avoid

- Turning the drill into an essay.
