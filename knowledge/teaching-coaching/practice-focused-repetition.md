# Focused Repetition → Deliberate / Fluency Practice

## Current Studigo definition

**Instruction:** "Use straightforward repetition when automaticity is the goal. Keep the target narrow, generate varied but equivalent items, give immediate feedback, and stop or reteach when the same error repeats."

## Real-world alignment

This is much closer to **deliberate practice and guided fluency practice** than to undifferentiated drill.

The defining safeguards are already in the code: narrow target, equivalent variation, feedback, and a stop/reteach rule when errors repeat.

## Definition

Focused repetition repeats a specific skill under controlled variation until execution becomes accurate and efficient, while feedback prevents repeated error from becoming practiced error.

## Core sequence

1. Confirm the learner understands the procedure/concept.
2. Define one narrow target.
3. Present a small run of equivalent but non-identical items.
4. Give immediate corrective feedback.
5. Track the error pattern.
6. If the same error repeats, stop and reteach.
7. After stability, mix the skill with alternatives or transfer contexts.

## Best-fit use cases

- Arithmetic facts/procedures.
- Symbol manipulation.
- Spelling/grammar conventions.
- Vocabulary production.
- Any skill where speed/automaticity frees attention for higher-level reasoning.

## Cautions

- Repeating misunderstanding is counterproductive.
- Accuracy comes before speed.
- Large item counts are not inherently evidence-based.
- Once the skill is fluent in isolation, blocked repetition should give way to discrimination/transfer practice.

## Example

For multiplication by 7, Studigo gives varied facts one at a time, corrects specific errors, and stops to reteach a pattern if the same fact family repeatedly fails. Once stable, the facts are mixed with other multiplication families.

## Recommended coaching rules

- Keep the target explicit.
- Use short sets with visible feedback.
- Do not reward rushing at the cost of accuracy.
- Stop if errors are systematic.
- End with a mixed or transfer check.
