# Transfer Practice → Varied / Interleaved Practice

## Current Studigo definition

**Instruction:** "After a learner can perform the modeled skill, vary the surface features and context so they must choose and apply the method, not just copy a pattern."

## Real-world alignment

This closely aligns with research on **transfer of learning**, **varied practice**, and—when multiple problem types are mixed—**interleaved practice**.

The important distinction is between repeating a procedure when its identity is obvious and recognizing **when** a procedure applies in a changed context.

## Definition

Transfer practice changes context, representation, surface details, or competing strategies after initial learning so the learner must identify the underlying structure and select an appropriate method.

## Core sequence

1. Verify the base skill can be performed.
2. Change a nonessential surface feature.
3. Require the learner to select the strategy.
4. Mix in an alternative strategy/problem type when appropriate.
5. Ask why the chosen method applies.
6. Increase distance from the original example gradually.
7. If performance collapses, return to a closer variation before retrying farther transfer.

## Best-fit use cases

- Cumulative mathematics practice.
- Applying formulas without the problem announcing which formula to use.
- Grammar rules in new sentences.
- Scientific principles in unfamiliar scenarios.
- Reading/writing strategies across texts.

## Cautions

- Transfer is not automatic.
- Far transfer is especially context-sensitive; do not promise generalization from one example.
- Interleaving too early can overwhelm a learner who has not acquired the component skills.
- Surface variation should preserve the target structure.

## Example

After a learner masters area of a triangle in blocked practice, Studigo mixes triangle, rectangle, and circle problems without naming the required formula. The learner must identify the shape/relationship first, then solve and explain why the formula applies.

## Recommended coaching rules

- Establish the base skill before increasing contextual distance.
- Vary one or two features at first.
- Require strategy selection, not only answer production.
- Mix competing methods once each is individually available.
- Explain a failed transfer as evidence about generalization, not as proof that the original learning was worthless.
