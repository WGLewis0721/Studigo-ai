# Singapore Math-Inspired → CPA + Model Method

## Current Studigo definition

**Description:** Concrete, pictorial, abstract.

**Instruction:** "For math, move deliberately from concrete objects or a bar/model representation to a drawing and then symbols. Ask the learner to connect each representation before increasing complexity."

## Real-world alignment

This is a strong match to documented Singapore mathematics practice, particularly:

- **Concrete–Pictorial–Abstract (CPA)** progression;
- the **Model Method / bar modeling** for representing quantitative relationships;
- a curriculum framework centered on mathematical problem solving.

Singapore's Ministry of Education explicitly describes CPA as movement from tangible materials to pictorial representations and then abstract symbols. Singapore's National Institute of Education teaches the sequence as part of primary mathematics teacher development.

## Definition

A mathematical concept is developed through connected representations: physical/tangible experience, visual representation, then symbolic notation. Bar/model representations can make part-whole, comparison, ratio, and unknown relationships visible before formal algebra.

## Core sequence

1. Identify the mathematical relationship.
2. Represent it concretely when useful.
3. Draw or model the same relationship.
4. Ask the learner to explain what each part represents.
5. Express the relationship symbolically.
6. Solve.
7. Check the symbolic solution against the representation.
8. Increase complexity after conceptual connection is demonstrated.

## Best-fit use cases

- Number sense.
- Place value.
- Fractions.
- Ratios.
- Word problems.
- Part-whole/comparison relationships.
- Early algebra.

## Cautions

- "Singapore Math" is broader than CPA alone.
- Do not use bar models mechanically when another representation is clearer.
- Avoid treating country-level achievement as proof that one method alone causes outcomes.
- Preserve the teacher's required notation and method.

## Example

**Word problem:** Maya has 18 stickers. Luis has 7 more than Maya. How many does Luis have?

Studigo can show 18 objects/count, then two bars with Luis's bar extending 7 beyond Maya's, then write `18 + 7 = 25`. The learner must explain how the extra segment maps to "+7."

## Recommended coaching rules

- Make representation-to-symbol mapping explicit.
- Use bar models for relationships, not decoration.
- Ask the learner to explain the representation.
- Return to a representation if symbolic manipulation becomes meaningless.
- Move forward once the learner demonstrates the connection.
