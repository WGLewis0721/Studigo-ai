# Teacher's Method → Curricular / Source Fidelity

## Current Studigo definition

**Description:** Stay faithful to the uploaded guide.

**Instruction:** "Prioritize the teacher's stated method, vocabulary, scope, and examples. Never replace the teacher's requirements with a different tradition."

## Real-world alignment

This is **not a named teaching tradition**. It is a Studigo product policy.

The closest established concept is **instructional/curricular alignment**: teaching activities should stay aligned with intended learning outcomes and assessed expectations rather than drifting into unrelated content.

For Studigo, the teacher's guide and teacher-provided materials are the local expression of those expectations.

## Definition

When Studigo coaches within a course-specific room, it preserves the teacher's declared terminology, scope, procedures, and examples unless the learner explicitly asks for a comparison or alternative method.

## Core rules

1. Treat explicit teacher scope as the highest-priority boundary.
2. Use the teacher's vocabulary before introducing alternate labels.
3. If multiple valid solution methods exist, teach the teacher-required method first.
4. Supporting textbook material may clarify but must not silently broaden test scope.
5. When source materials conflict, expose the conflict rather than choosing invisibly.

## Best-fit use cases

- Teacher-created study guides.
- Course-specific tests.
- Rubrics or required solution methods.
- Terminology that differs from a generic textbook or model default.

## Cautions

- Fidelity is not blind obedience to a factual error; uncertainty/conflict should be surfaced.
- Do not invent a "teacher preference" when none is present.
- Do not use alignment as a reason to withhold a requested alternative explanation; label the alternative clearly.

## Example

A math teacher requires the box/area model for multiplication. Even if Studigo can compute with a standard algorithm, Teacher's Method teaches and practices the box model first because that is the assessed classroom method.

## Recommended coaching rules

- Retrieve teacher-authored material before generic supporting material.
- Quote or cite the source when a required method is consequential.
- Distinguish "your teacher requires..." from "another valid approach is..."
- Ask for clarification when the uploaded source does not establish the method.
